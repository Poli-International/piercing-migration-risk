# Piercing Migration & Rejection Risk: user guide

Evaluate anatomical, jewellery, and mechanical risk factors that influence piercing migration and rejection, log millimeter measurements over time, and distinguish normal healing from active rejection.

## What it is for

The tool helps individuals and studios evaluate whether a piercing is healing normally, irritated, or migrating toward the surface. By assessing tissue architecture, post sizing, materials, friction, aftercare routines, and healing history, it identifies physical forces acting on the channel. It also provides an objective measurement log where clients record repeatable millimeter values over time to share with a piercer.

## Who it is for

This tool is designed for:

- Clients with healing or mature piercings who notice extra bar showing or redness and want to evaluate stability.
- Professional piercers seeking an educational reference to explain post downsizing and tissue architecture.
- Studio apprentices learning how tissue tension, barbell geometry, and materials influence channel stability.

## How to use it

### Urgent Embedding Callout

1. Review the alert banner titled `Jewellery Embedding is Urgent and Different from Migration`.
2. Distinguish slow migration over months from acute embedding, where swelling swallows jewellery within days.
3. If ends sink into swollen tissue, seek immediate professional assistance for emergency removal or upsizing.

### Risk Factor Assessment

1. Open `Risk Factor Assessment`.
2. Complete all six categories: `1. Piercing Placement & Tissue Architecture`, `2. Jewellery Length & Fit`, `3. Jewellery Material & Biocompatibility`, `4. Friction, Snagging & Pressure`, `5. Aftercare & Handling Routine`, and `6. Healing History & Tissue Condition`.
3. Click `Evaluate Risk Factors`. To reset selections, click `Clear All Selections`.

### Factor Assessment Results & Actionable Steps

1. Review the `Factor Assessment Results` card showing your overall tendency: `Overall Tendency: Lower Likelihood of Migration`, `Overall Tendency: Moderate / Mixed Risk`, or `Overall Tendency: Higher Likelihood of Migration / Rejection`.
2. Inspect items listed under factors raising your risk alongside protective elements.
3. Review tailored steps under `What You Can Change (Actionable Steps)` to plan adjustments.

### Repeatable Measurement Log: Objective Evidence

1. Locate `Repeatable Measurement Log: Objective Evidence`.
2. Select your `Measurement Method:` between `Tissue Bridge: Distance from hole to hole / tissue edge (mm)` and `Visible Bar: Length of exposed bar between skin and beads (mm)`.
3. Set the `Date of Measurement:`, measure with a millimeter ruler or caliper, and enter the value in `Measurement (mm):`.
4. Check applicable events under `What changed since last time? (Select all that apply):` and add optional notes in `Notes (optional):`.
5. Click `Save Measurement`. Use `Delete` to remove an entry, or click `Clear Measurement History` to erase all entries.

### Signs of Migration, Illustrated

1. Scroll to `Signs of Migration, Illustrated`.
2. Inspect the interactive comparison diagrams for `1. Thinning Skin Over the Bar`, `2. Bar Visible Through the Skin`, `3. Entry and Exit Holes Moving Closer Together`, and `4. Redness & Irritation That Does Not Settle`.
3. Toggle between `Normal / Settled` and `Migrating / Shallow` buttons above each diagram to see how tissue depth and redness alter.

### Surface & Flat Placements Explained

1. Read the mechanical guidance in `Surface & Flat Placements Explained`.
2. Learn why flat tissue planes experience continuous lateral tension without a protective fold.
3. Review why surface bars and single-point dermal anchors have a finite lifespan compared to through-and-through piercings.

### Per-Placement Guidance

1. Click any tab in `Per-Placement Guidance`: `Navel Piercing`, `Eyebrow Piercing`, `Surface Barbell`, or `Dermal Anchor`.
2. Review anatomical vulnerabilities, recommended jewellery geometries, and warning signs specific to that site.

### What a Piercer Can Actually Do

1. Read options in `What a Piercer Can Actually Do`: `1. Downsize to a Shorter Post`, `2. Switch Jewellery Style, Tops, or Material`, `3. Remove and Resite (Repierce Later)`, and `4. The Honest Reality: An Actively Rejecting Piercing Usually Comes Out`.
2. Understand when downsizing stabilizes an irritated channel and when timely removal is necessary to prevent scarring.

### After a Piercing Rejects

1. Review guidance in `After a Piercing Rejects`.
2. Learn why allowing a piercing to reject completely through the skin creates a split notch or deep scar.
3. Follow the provided link to explore scar care in the Keloid & Raised Scar Guide.

### Print Signs & Evidence Guide

1. Click `Print Signs & Evidence Guide` in the top header.
2. The print dialog renders the sheet titled `Piercing Migration Signs & Measurement Sheet`.
3. The printed page includes warning signs, assessment notes, and your logged measurement table to share with your piercer.

## What it does not do

This tool evaluates mechanical and anatomical risk factors, but it does not perform the following tasks:

- It does not calculate exact jewellery dimensions for your anatomy. For sizing calculations, use the [Jewelry Size Visualizer](https://poliinternational.com/jewelry-size-visualizer/).
- It does not generate daily saline cleaning schedules or healing timeline reminders. For care routines, use the [Aftercare Schedule Generator](https://poliinternational.com/aftercare-schedule-generator/).
- It does not measure needle angles or perpendicular channel alignment. For tissue angle assessments, use the [Piercing Angle Guide](https://poliinternational.com/piercing-angle-guide/).
- It does not diagnose or treat hypertrophic scars or keloids resulting from rejected piercings. For scar care, use the [Keloid & Raised Scar Guide](https://poliinternational.com/keloid-raised-scar-guide/).

## Where your data lives

All measurement entries and factor selections stay strictly inside your web browser on your current device. Data is stored locally using browser localStorage under the key `poli_migration_measurements`. Nothing is ever transmitted over a network or stored on a remote server.

Your data remains saved between visits on the same browser and device. It clears if you click `Clear Measurement History` within the tool, if you clear browser site storage, or if you use private browsing windows that wipe local data on exit. When you print or save to PDF, the export contains your logged measurements, recorded dates, change calculations, and selected physical events.

## Printing and exporting

You can generate a physical handout or PDF by clicking `Print Signs & Evidence Guide` at the top of the interface. Dedicated print styles format the document cleanly, hiding buttons and form controls.

The printed record includes the four primary warning signs of migration, educational evaluation notes, and a structured table of every logged millimeter measurement with baseline comparisons and observation notes. This sheet is formatted specifically to hand to a professional piercer during an in-person studio consultation.

## Questions and answers

### What is the difference between piercing migration and rejection?
Migration is the physical movement of a piercing channel away from its original placement, caused by tissue pressure, improper post length, or movement. Rejection is the final outcome of migration, where the body treats the jewellery as an intrusion and pushes it completely out through the skin. An irritated piercing showing early migration can sometimes be stabilized by downsizing, whereas an actively rejecting piercing cannot be reversed.

### How can I tell if my piercing is migrating or just irritated?
An irritated piercing typically presents with localized swelling, redness around the holes, or a fluid bump, while the distance between holes remains unchanged. Migration specifically involves tissue loss, characterized by a thinning skin bridge, more exposed bar without swelling, or holes moving closer together.

### Can an actively rejecting piercing be saved?
If a piercing has experienced substantial tissue thinning and the body is actively pushing the bar outward, it will not regenerate lost skin or reverse direction. While early intervention such as downsizing can relieve mechanical levering, an advanced rejecting piercing should be removed by a piercer to avoid severe scarring.

### Why do surface piercings reject more often than earlobe or cartilage piercings?
Earlobe and cartilage piercings pass through natural tissue folds where opposing skin walls hold the jewellery under balanced tension. Surface bars and dermal anchors sit in flat skin planes under constant lateral tension as muscles flex and clothing rubs against the area. Without a supportive fold, the body naturally expels rigid foreign objects along the path of least resistance.

### When should I downsize my piercing jewellery?
Initial piercing posts are deliberately long to allow for normal inflammatory swelling during early healing. Once swelling settles, an overly long post snags on clothing, tilts, and levers the channel. Visit your piercer to downsize to a snug, flush-fitting post as soon as initial swelling subsides.

### What should I do if my piercing jewellery is embedding into my skin?
Jewellery embedding occurs when swelling causes the skin to swallow the decorative ends, disks, or beads, representing an acute emergency. If you notice tissue growing over any part of your jewellery, visit a professional piercer or healthcare provider immediately to replace it with a longer post before the skin seals.

## Limits

This tool provides educational risk factor analysis and tracking based on established professional practice; it cannot assess real-time cellular pathology, diagnose active infections, or evaluate internal tissue vascularity. It cannot replace an in-person physical examination by a qualified professional piercer using sterile instrumentation, tactile assessment, and specialized lighting.

Only an experienced professional piercer or licensed medical practitioner can physically examine your anatomy, determine whether sufficient tissue depth exists to sustain a piercing, assess whether an inflamed channel is salvageable, or safely remove deeply embedded jewellery. If you experience severe throbbing pain, spreading heat, green or foul-smelling discharge, red streaks, or systemic fever, seek immediate medical evaluation.
